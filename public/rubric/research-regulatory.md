## 1. NVAO Assessment Framework 2018 — Standard 3 (assessment) & Standard 4 (achieved learning outcomes)

URL: https://www.nvao.net/files/attachments/.139/Assessment_Framework_for_the_Higher_Education_Accreditation_System_of_the_Netherlands_2018.pdf
(NL original: https://www.nvao.net/files/attachments/.89/Beoordelingskader_accreditatiestelsel_hoger_onderwijs_Nederland_2018.pdf)

Extensive/limited programme assessment, quoted verbatim from the PDF (p.20–21 of the assessment-framework chapter):

> **Standard 3: The programme has an adequate system of student assessment in place.**
> The student assessments are valid, reliable and sufficiently independent. The requirements are transparent to the students. The quality of interim and final examinations is sufficiently safeguarded and meets the statutory quality standards. The tests support the students' own learning processes.

> **Standard 4: The programme demonstrates that the intended learning outcomes are achieved.**
> The achievement of the intended learning outcomes is demonstrated by the results of tests, the final projects, and the performance of graduates in actual practice or in postgraduate programmes.

> As a rule, standard 4 is not addressed in an initial accreditation assessment. The panel will only assess this standard if, in the opinion of NVAO, the procedure involves an existing programme and final projects are available to be assessed. … In the information dossier, the programme must describe how it has tested the achievement of the learning outcomes. Such tests may be based on various products or examinations that are summarised here in the concept of a final project.

I grepped the full 2,391-line text export for "rubric," "second examiner," "two examiners," and "independent" — **the word "rubric" does not appear anywhere in the framework**, and there is no explicit requirement for a second/independent examiner on final projects or for written substantiation of individual grades. "Independent" is used throughout only in the sense of the NVAO assessment *panel's* independence, not of double-marking student work.

**Implies:** The NVAO framework is deliberately generic/principle-based ("valid, reliable, sufficiently independent, transparent"). It neither mandates rubrics nor double examiners nor written grade justification for graduation projects — it leaves the *how* to the institution, and only checks that final projects demonstrate the intended learning outcomes were achieved. A rubric-based redesign is fully **permitted**, not required, by NVAO; it is one reasonable way to satisfy "valid, reliable, transparent."

## 2 & 3. TU Delft Assessment Framework (Toetskader) 2023–2028 and rubric guidance

Main page: https://www.tudelft.nl/en/teaching-support/educational-advice/assess/guidelines/assessment-framework
Appendices: https://www.tudelft.nl/en/teaching-support/educational-advice/assess/guidelines/assessment-framework/appendices
Faculty policies (condition 15, §4.4 graduation manual): https://www.tudelft.nl/en/teaching-support/educational-advice/assess/guidelines/assessment-framework/assessment-policies-in-the-faculty
Assessment competence (condition 24, graduation committee): https://www.tudelft.nl/en/teaching-support/educational-advice/assess/guidelines/assessment-framework/assessment-competence
Rubric design guide: https://www.tudelft.nl/en/teaching-support/educational-advice/assess/projects-and-assignments/design-rubric

Key points (paraphrased/quoted from the fetched pages):

- **Rubric definition:** "Matrix used to determine the score per criterion that students are assessed on. For all criteria (rows), the requirements for each level (columns) are described."
- **Condition 15 (graduation manuals):** programme directors must ensure an up-to-date graduation manual "in line with the programme assessment plan and with regulations. The manual contains the topics listed in [§]4.4."
- **§4.4 (graduation manual content):** must include "the rubric (synonym: grading guide) and assessment sheet," learning objectives and assessment criteria, and — per a footnote — the rubric needs "descriptors per criterion of the following two levels: a) the level of just sufficient (the equivalent of a 6) and b) the level of the learning objectives (the equivalent of a 10)."
- **Condition 24 (graduation committee):** "The boards of examiners have regulations on the composition of the graduation committee to ensure assessment quality of graduation projects" — TU Delft-level policy does **not** itself mandate two examiners; it delegates the composition rule to each faculty's Board of Examiners (references CEG's RGfBE as an example).
- **Grade determination/justification:** course assessment plans must show "how the final grade represents the performance of an individual student and how the learning objectives contribute to the programme's intended learning outcomes." The four-eyes principle is applied "during development of assessment plans, development of rubrics, and assessment of student work."
- **Rubric design guide (Q3):** recommends organizing as a matrix (criteria = rows, performance levels = columns), **3–5 criteria**, levels ordered worst→best (example shown: Insufficient/Sufficient/Good/Excellent), an **analytic** approach ("for each criterion, determine what a 'pass' performance should consist of; describe in 1–2 sentences"), a way to make grade derivation clear ("is it clear how the grade is derived?"; a "simple formula" is acceptable), and to keep the whole rubric to one A4 sheet. It does not prescribe whether to show points to students.

**Implies:** TU Delft's own Toetskader (2023–2028) already **requires** every graduation manual to contain a rubric/grading guide and assessment form aligned to learning objectives (condition 15/§4.4), and already specifies the two-level (6/10) descriptor structure. This is TU Delft-wide policy that predates and drives the IDE-specific 2026 rubric — the new IDE rubric is IDE's *implementation* of an existing, already-mandatory TU Delft requirement, not a novel invention.

## 4. IDE MSc TER 2025–2026 and RGfBE (same PDF)

Source: https://filelist.tudelft.nl/Studentenportal/Faculteitspecifiek/IO/Onderwijs/Regelgeving/MSc%20IDE%20TER%20and%20RGfBE%202025-2026.pdf (64 pp, downloaded and grepped in full; not cat'd).

- **"rubric" does not appear anywhere in the TER or RGfBE** — grading procedure for graduation is governed by generic examination articles, not a named rubric requirement.
- **Two examiners (oral exams, incl. graduation defence):**
  > Article 18 – Oral examinations … 2. An oral examination is held by at least two examiners. Deviation of this rule must be approved by the Board of Examiners. The course coordinator should therefor submit a substantiated request. In case the oral exam by one examiner is permitted … the oral exam should be recorded (audio and/or video) …
  > 3. … a final presentation of the Graduation Project is given publicly except in special cases …

  (Identical clause repeated in the RGfBE, Article 13.)
- **Grade transparency/justification:**
  > Article 29 … 2. The method of assessment will be transparent so that the student can ascertain how the result was reached.
- **Examiner mandate:** graduation supervisors are "mandated by the Board of Examiners to serve as examiners, not only of the graduation project, but also of the Master programme as a whole" (footnote 16, cross-referenced from the manual — the TER establishes the legal basis for supervisors acting as examiners).
- **Appeals:** Article 33 (TER) / Article 33 (RGfBE) — appeals against Board of Examiners/examiner decisions go to the Examination Appeals Board (CBE) for the Examinations, via TU Delft Legal Affairs; objections against other decisions go to the TU Delft students' objection committee.
- No explicit clause on "deviation from a rubric" exists (because the TER/RGfBE never names a rubric) — deviations are handled generically via the Board of Examiners' Article-by-Article deviation powers (e.g., Art. 13/18 for two-examiner deviation, Art. 29.3 for degree-audit requirement deviation).

**Implies:** The TER/RGfBE **permits** a rubric-based graduation assessment (nothing conflicts with it) and **already requires** two examiners for the oral graduation defence and transparent, ascertainable grade justification — both of which a rubric satisfies well — but it does **not itself mandate a rubric**; that obligation comes from the TU Delft Toetskader (Q2) and is operationalised in the graduation manual (Q5), not the TER.

## 5. IDE MSc Graduation student manual — existing rubric & the "from February 2026" note

Source: https://filelist.tudelft.nl/Studentenportal/Faculteitspecifiek/IO/Onderwijs/graduation/Downloads/MyCase%20afstuderen/IDE%20MSc%20Graduation%20student%20manual.pdf (downloaded and grepped in full).

This is the most directly relevant document — **TU Delft/IDE already runs a rubric + assessment form for graduation and is already mid-transition to a revised one**:

> §2.4.1 Assessment criteria: "…The assessment of each learning objective is based on predefined criteria and a scale, which together merge to the **Assessment Rubric**. The Assessment Form and Rubric can be found in Appendix A and as a download on the Graduation website. For students who started their master's programme in September 2024 or later (graduation codes IDEM1000, IDEM2000 and IDEM3000) **a revised assessment form and rubric apply, which will be available from February 2026.**"

> Learning objectives section: "…For students who started their master's programme in September 2024 or later (graduation codes IDEM1000, IDEM2000 and IDEM3000), revised learning objectives will apply and will be available from February 2026."

> §7.3 (Graduation Ceremony): supervisors "mutually assess your project using the course's rubric and fill out the assessment form (Appendix A)"; the public presentation "is part of the rubric and assessment"; the post-decision evaluation discusses "strengths and weaknesses of the work performed … by means of the assessment form and rubric."

> Appendix A header: "This Rubric and Assessment form applies to students enrolled in ID4190-16, ID4290-16 and ID4390-16. For students who started their master's programme in September 2024 or later (graduation codes IDEM1000, IDEM2000 and IDEM3000) a revised Rubric and Assessment form appl[y] and will be available from February 2026."

> Footnote 16: "Your TU Delft supervisors (responsible supervisor and supervisor) are mandated by the Board of Examiners to serve as examiners, not only of the graduation project, but also of the Master programme as a whole."

**Implies:** There is **already** a live rubric + assessment-form regime for IDE graduation projects (old course codes ID4190/4290/4390-16), and IDE has already publicly committed, in the current manual, to a **revised** rubric/assessment form/learning-objectives set for the new graduation codes (IDEM1000/2000/3000, Sept-2024-cohort-onward) "available from February 2026." The 23-Sep-2026 rubric this project is building is very likely *that* announced revision (or a direct successor to it) — it is expected/pre-committed by IDE's own manual, not an ad hoc change. Worth reconciling the Feb-2026 date already in the manual with the 23-Sep-2026 in-force date given to this project — either the manual's date slipped, or the Sept rubric is a further iteration after a Feb 2026 interim version.
