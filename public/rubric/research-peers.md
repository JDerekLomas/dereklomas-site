# Peer benchmarking: MSc graduation/thesis assessment at comparable design schools

Research date: 2026-09-18. Context: TU Delft IDE's new rubric (8 LOs, 19 descriptors, 4 levels,
hidden points 2/4/7/11, equal weights, published points→grade formula, two supervisors score
independently then reconcile, deviation from computed grade must be justified) vs. the old 2018
rubric (explicit feasibility/desirability/viability, visible grade, holistic team judgement).

Sources are primary documents where publicly available (downloaded and grepped), otherwise the
best public secondary description, flagged as such.

---

## 1. TU Eindhoven — Industrial Design, Final Master Project (FMP)

- URL (rubric booklet, v1.5, 2019/20 — covers Bachelor + Master, FMP section starts p.70):
  https://www.cursor.tue.nl/fileadmin/user_upload/nieuws/2020/12_december/week_2/LR_ID_issues/1920_Rubrics_-_Version_1.5.pdf
- Programme page: https://educationguide.tue.nl/programs/graduate-school/masters-programs/industrial-design/curriculum/final-master-project
- **Criteria / levels**: FMP is scored on 4 topics (Overall Competence of Designing, Scientific &
  Professional Skills, Vision & Identity, Expertise Areas), each with sub-criteria (e.g. under OCD:
  Integration of Expertise Areas, Design & Research Processes, Demonstrator). **4 levels**:
  Excellent / Good / Sufficient / Insufficient.
- **Grade visible?** Not a numeric formula — result is a **verdict** (P/E for pass variants, H or
  C for fail/resit conditions depending on which topic is missed), i.e. pass/fail-oriented, not a
  0–10 number computed from the rubric.
- **Feasibility/desirability/viability or aesthetics explicit?** Not FDV-framed, but "Creativity &
  Aesthetics" is one of 7 parallel Expertise Areas scored in Master Year 2 courses; the FMP itself
  emphasizes design/research process integration and a working "Demonstrator" (well-engineered /
  experiential / museum-quality communication) rather than desirability-feasibility-viability.
- **Independent scoring?** Assessed by an **FMP panel of two examiners + a plenary session**
  (changed from three examiners in the 2018/19 revision) — not independent-then-reconciled in the
  TU Delft IDE sense; more a joint panel discussion.
- **Aggregation**: Holistic verdict per topic, not a summed/weighted formula.
- **Deviation justification required?** No formula to deviate from — the rubric levels are fixed
  per the document ("Examination Moment: the topics, criteria... are fixed"), but combination into
  the final verdict is examiner judgement, not arithmetic.

## 2. University of Twente — Industrial Design Engineering (IDE)

- No IDE-specific published thesis rubric found (checked utwente.nl/en/ide, student charter, NVAO
  accreditation report). UT's **Educational Science & Technology (EST)** master's programme
  publishes a directly comparable "Master's Thesis Rubric and Grading Form," which follows UT's
  house style and is the best available proxy for how UT structures thesis grading:
  https://www.utwente.nl/en/est/masterest/final-project-trajectory-started-in-2025-2026/assessment-form-and-rubric-final-project-est.pdf
- **Criteria / levels**: 7 weighted components (Problem Definition 10%, Theoretical Framework 15%,
  Research Design & Methodology 15%, Analysis & Results 15%, Conclusions & Discussion 15%,
  Process & Agency 20%, Oral Presentation & Defense 10%) — **numeric weights, not equal**. Each
  component scored 1–10 in decimals against listed criteria (no fixed discrete "levels" grid like
  TU Delft/TNW/TU/e — closer to free-form 1–10 marking guided by bullet criteria).
- **Grade visible?** Yes — explicit **weighted-average formula** to a rounded final grade, with a
  published rounding table (e.g. ≥n.25 and <n.75 rounds to n.5).
- **Threshold rule**: all 7 components must individually score ≥5.5 to pass, regardless of the
  weighted average.
- **FDV/aesthetics explicit?** No — criteria are generic research/thesis criteria (problem
  definition, literature, method, analysis, conclusions), not FDV or aesthetics.
- **Independent scoring?** Supervisor and Second Reader both score; "Process & Agency" (20%) can
  **only** be scored by the supervisor, not the second reader — a partial-independence model, not
  full independent-then-reconcile.
- **Deviation justification?** Not applicable — grade is a direct formula output with per-component
  minimum thresholds; the rounding rule is itself the only "deviation" mechanic.

## 3. Aalto University — School of Arts, Design and Architecture (covers Collaborative & Industrial
   Design MA)

- URL (Master's Thesis Guide 2022, Appendix 1 — evaluation criteria):
  https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2025-10/ARTS-Maisterin-opinnaytteen-arviointikriteetit-2022-EN.pdf
- Guide: https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2022-11/Aalto%20ARTS%20Master's%20Thesis%20Guide%202022.pdf
- **Criteria / levels**: ~7 criterion groups (Choice of topic & goals; Command of topic & sources;
  Discussion/conclusions; **Quality of the artistic component** — explicitly skippable for
  theoretical/applied-research theses; Description of working process; Quality of language &
  presentation; Knowledge & professional skills; Overall impression). **6 levels**: fail(0) / 1 /
  2 / 3 / 4 / 5, with "grades 3–5, the previous standard is included in the next" (cumulative
  descriptors).
- **Grade visible?** Yes, single overall grade 0–5 — but **explicitly holistic**: "the final grade
  is given for the thesis as a whole using those evaluation criteria which are relevant for the
  thesis," with instruction to drop/de-emphasize criteria (e.g. skip "artistic component" for
  research-oriented theses). No summing formula.
- **FDV/aesthetics explicit?** "Quality of the artistic component" (idea, execution, artistic/design
  vision) is a first-class named criterion for design/art theses — closer to a desirability/form
  axis than TU Delft's old FDV triad, but not feasibility/viability framed.
- **Independent scoring?** Thesis is examined by an assigned examiner and graded by the Programme
  Committee based on the examiner's statement — not two-independent-scorers-then-reconcile.
- **Deviation justification?** N/A (no formula to deviate from); process delays not the student's
  fault are explicitly named as mitigating factors a grader may weigh in.

## 4. Royal College of Art / Imperial College — Innovation Design Engineering (IDE)

- No public rubric or marking-criteria document found (checked rca.ac.uk, ide.rca.ac.uk,
  imperial.ac.uk/design-engineering, Imperial exam regulations).
- What is public: Imperial's **university-wide** Taught Masters classification bands — Pass 50%,
  Merit 60%, Distinction 70%, with an uplift provision (68.00–69.99 on the dissertation/final
  major project can be uplifted to Distinction).
  https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/faculty-of-engineering/computing/public/current-students-msc-regulations/2020-to-2021/MSc-Computing-Regulations.pdf
  (Computing-specific doc, cited as representative of the Imperial percentage-band house style;
  IDE's own regs were not found public.)
- Inference: RCA/Imperial (like most UK art/design postgrad programmes) almost certainly uses
  percentage marks moderated by an external examiner against Pass/Merit/Distinction descriptors,
  not a points-rubric with a published formula. This is typical of UK design education generally —
  criteria exist in internal handbooks, not published online.

## 5. Stanford d.school / ME310 capstone

- No public grading rubric found (checked me.stanford.edu, ME310 project page, Applied Innovation).
  ME310 (https://me.stanford.edu/project-opportunity-me310abc) is described only at the level of
  process (problem redefinition → prototyping → "reference model" in June); grading criteria are
  in internal syllabi, not published.
- Note for synthesis: Stanford d.school/ME310 is widely cited as the **origin** of the
  desirability–feasibility–viability (human/business/technical) framing that TU Delft IDE's *old*
  2018 rubric used explicitly — but Stanford does not publish that framing as a numeric grading
  instrument.

## 6. Dutch universities with published points→grade rubric formulas (peer benchmark within NL)

### TU Delft, Mechanical Engineering (ME) — MSc Thesis Grading Rubric
- URL: https://filelist.tudelft.nl/Studentenportal/Faculteitspecifiek/ME%20-%20new/Onderwijs/Master%20studenten/MSc%20formulieren/2024-25/MSc%20thesis%20Grading%20Rubric_versie%20Sept_2023.pdf
- **~15 criteria** across 4 groups (Content, Communication, Process, [implicit overall]), **5
  levels**: Excellent (9–10) / Good (8) / Satisfactory (7) / Sufficient (6) / Insufficient (<6).
- Grade **visible** (9–10, 8, 7, 6, <6 bands directly map to Dutch 1–10 grades).
- No FDV or aesthetics criteria (engineering research criteria: theoretical knowledge, new
  knowledge generation, research/design significance, report quality, presentation, defense,
  supervision contact, responsibility, critical attitude, open-mindedness).
- **Not independent-then-reconciled**: signed off by a **graduation committee** (chair + up to 4
  members incl. cum laude assessor) who jointly "agree with the final grade" after the defense.
- **No published arithmetic formula** — rubric is diagnostic/formative per criterion; final grade
  is committee consensus, not a sum.

### TU Delft, Aerospace Engineering (AE) — MSc Thesis Rubric
- URL: https://filelist.tudelft.nl/Studentenportal/Faculteitspecifiek/LR/Onderwijs/Thesis/Rubrics_MSc_thesis_AE-2022-2023%20and%20after.pdf
- **5 weighted criteria**, 6 levels each: Content & scientific/engineering approach (30%), Report
  (20%), Presentation & defence (20%), Creativity & initiative (15%), Project management (15%).
- Grade **visible**, and this one *does* have a **published formula**: final grade = weighted
  average of the five criteria, rounded to the nearest half point — the clearest Dutch engineering
  precedent for "formula-computed grade," though weights are unequal (30/20/20/15/15) vs. IDE's
  equal-weight design.
- No FDV/aesthetics framing (engineering-research criteria).

### TU Delft, TNW (Applied Sciences) — Master Thesis (MEP) Grading Scheme
- URL: https://filelist.tudelft.nl/TNW/Afdelingen/ChemE/CE/Education/TNW-MEP-Grading-Scheme.pdf
- **~13 criteria** in 5 groups (Scientific knowledge & method, Method & approach, Report,
  Presentation & defence, Competences), **6 levels** per criterion (fail through 10, with explicit
  "in-between adjacent descriptions" half-steps) — structurally the closest Delft analogue to IDE's
  "hidden points between levels" idea, but visible/numeric rather than hidden.
- Grade visible. **Explicitly not a fixed-weight average**: "The final grade is based on the
  sub-grades... but it is not an average with fixed weights"; "personal contribution" is
  double-weighted informally. So TNW is the one Delft peer that **deliberately rejects** a formula
  in favor of examiner judgement anchored by the rubric.

### Wageningen University — MSc Thesis Rubric v4.0
- URL: https://backend.wur.nl/sites/default/files/2025-11/Rubric-MSc-thesis-v4.0-2022-2023.pdf
- **4 weighted categories**, many sub-criteria: Performance (40%: independence/initiative,
  commitment, feedback-handling...), Research report (50%), Oral presentation (5%), Oral defence
  (5%). **5 levels** (Unacceptable/Insufficient/Just sufficient/Good/Exemplary) each mapped to a
  **paired grade band** (2–3, 4–5, 6–7, 8–9, 10) — i.e. Wageningen already uses something close to
  TU Delft IDE's "level → point range" idea, but the point ranges (and the resulting grade) are
  **visible on the form**, not hidden. Both assessors score independently on a shared rubric, then
  the examiner (usually with the co-promotor) sets the final grade — closer to TU Delft IDE's
  independent-then-reconcile model than any other peer found.
- No FDV/aesthetics (agricultural/life-science research criteria).

## Synthesis: is TU Delft IDE's new design typical, unusual, or extreme?

Feature-by-feature against the 7 peers actually documented above:

| Feature | TU Delft IDE (new) | How common among peers |
|---|---|---|
| Rubric with defined levels | Yes | **Typical** — every peer found uses a rubric (TU/e, UT-EST, Aalto, TUD-ME/AE/TNW, Wageningen all have one) |
| Points/grade **hidden** until computed | Yes (2/4/7/11) | **Extreme / unique.** Every other rubric found — Dutch or international — shows the grade band or points directly on the form (Wageningen's paired bands, TNW's 6-level numeric grid, AE's %, ME's 9-10/8/7/6/<6). Hiding the points appears to be a genuine outlier, not standard Dutch practice. |
| **Equal weights** across all criteria | Yes (8 LOs, equal) | **Unusual.** Every weighted peer found uses *unequal* weights reflecting judged importance: Wageningen (40/50/5/5), UT-EST (10/15/15/15/15/20/10), TUD-AE (30/20/20/15/15). TNW explicitly rejects fixed weights altogether. Equal weighting across 8 heterogeneous learning objectives has no precedent in this sample. |
| Published **formula** grade = f(points) | Yes | **Rare but not unique.** TUD-AE (weighted average, rounded) and UT-EST (weighted average with threshold + rounding table) do this. But most peers (TU/e, TNW, TUD-ME, Aalto) explicitly keep the final grade a **committee/examiner judgement**, using the rubric as structure/evidence rather than an arithmetic input. TNW's rubric says outright "it is not an average with fixed weights." So a strict formula is a minority approach even within TU Delft's own engineering faculties. |
| **Independent** scoring by 2 examiners, then reconciliation | Yes | **Closest peer: Wageningen** (two independent assessors on shared rubric, examiner reconciles). UT-EST has partial independence (2nd reader can't score one 20% component). Most others (TU/e's panel+plenary, TUD-ME's committee sign-off, Aalto's single examiner + committee) are joint/consensus models, not independent-then-reconcile. |
| Deviation from computed grade must be justified | Yes | **No peer found requires this**, because no peer found treats the rubric output as binding in the first place — where a formula exists (AE, UT-EST) it simply *is* the grade, no deviation contemplated; where it doesn't (TU/e, TNW, ME, Aalto), the "deviation" question doesn't arise since judgement was never subordinated to arithmetic. This pairing — computed grade as *default* that examiners may only leave with justification — is the most distinctive procedural feature of the new IDE rubric. |
| Feasibility/Desirability/Viability explicit | Old IDE rubric only | **Not used by name anywhere else** in this sample. Aalto comes closest with a named "Quality of the artistic component / design vision" criterion; TU/e names "Creativity & Aesthetics" as one of 7 parallel expertise areas and a "Demonstrator" criterion (well-engineered / experiential / museum-quality) in the FMP itself. FDV as a triad is a d.school/IDEO-lineage framework by reputation (not confirmed in any public Stanford rubric), and TU Delft IDE's old rubric was unusual for making it an explicit *grading* criterion rather than a process/ideation heuristic. Dropping it from the new rubric brings IDE structurally closer to engineering-thesis peers (TUD-ME/AE/TNW, Wageningen, UT), all of which grade generic research/design-process competencies rather than FDV or aesthetics. |

**Bottom line**: TU Delft IDE's new rubric is **typical** in having a multi-criterion, multi-level
rubric at all, and in dropping explicit FDV/aesthetics criteria in favor of generic learning-outcome
competencies (this actually converges it with engineering-faculty and Wageningen practice). It is
**unusual** in using strictly equal weights across 8 objectives — no peer does this; weighting by
judged importance (or explicitly rejecting fixed weights, as TNW does) is the norm. It is
**extreme relative to every peer sampled** on two specific mechanics: (1) hiding the point values
behind the levels rather than showing them, and (2) making a formula-computed grade the enforced
default that requires written justification to depart from. Wageningen and TUD-Aerospace are the
closest real precedents for "rubric feeds a formula," but both keep the numbers visible and neither
treats deviation as something to be justified — because in neither case is deviation from the
formula contemplated as a normal, available action for examiners.
