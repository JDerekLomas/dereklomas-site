# Grade inflation and grade distributions in Dutch master's theses — research notes

Prepared 2026-09-18 for the TU Delft IDE graduation-rubric page. Testing the supervisor's claim:
**"about a third of IDE graduation projects now get a 9 or higher; it should be more like 20%."**

---

## 1. Published data on Dutch thesis/grade inflation generally

No Dutch Inspectorate (Onderwijsinspectie) report was found that publishes a *specific numeric thesis-grade distribution*. What exists is qualitative/trend reporting plus one hard TU Delft number (see §5).

- **ScienceGuide, May 2025**, "[Cijferinflatie aan universiteiten neemt toe en maakt diploma's minder waard](https://www.scienceguide.nl/2025/05/cijferinflatie-aan-universiteiten-neemt-toe-en-maakt-diplomas-minder-waard/)" — reports that university (wo) grades have "gradually risen" over the past ten years while international measurements (e.g. PISA-linked skill measures) show incoming-student skill levels declining. Source cited: researchers from Leuven and Maastricht. Full article is paywalled (SURFconext); no percentage figures were retrievable.
- **Mare Online (Leiden), "Generatie 2020" series**:
  - [Part 2](https://www.mareonline.nl/achtergrond/generatie-2020-deel-2-hoe-een-zes-de-nieuwe-zeven-werd-en-een-vijf-het-genadezesje) quotes an instructor: *"A 7 is the new 6 ... professors grade more leniently, as exams remain largely unchanged."* Gives Dutch HE structural data: student:staff ratio worsened from 1:17.3 (2003) to 1:23.6 (2019) (+61% students, +18.6% staff); funding per student fell from €20,100 (2000) to €15,200 (2017), a 25% real-terms cut. No thesis-specific numbers.
  - [Part 3](https://www.mareonline.nl/achtergrond/generatie-2020-deel-3-waarom-de-universitaire-bul-een-statusbrevetje-is-geworden) is the most directly relevant piece found: it cites the **Onderwijsinspectie and NVAO** as reporting that fewer than 5% of programmes undergo formal remedial ("herstel") trajectories, and that NVAO accreditation panels review "vijftien willekeurige afstudeerscripties" (15 random theses) per assessment. Leiden professor **Bruno Verbeek** (quoted): *"Waar je vroeger ieder jaar wel een paar scripties had die op wat redactiewerk na direct publiceerbaar waren, is dat steeds minder het geval: van de scripties die ik de afgelopen vijf jaar heb begeleid of als tweede lezer heb bekeken, was er daar maar één van."* He explicitly attributes this to "cijferinflatie" — thesis grades rising while the underlying quality does not. Professor Remco Breuker, on supervision load: *"Op papier krijgen we tien minuten per student voor een scriptiegesprek."*
  - No UNL/VSNU sector-wide thesis-grade-distribution report was located in the time available; UvA is named (via AT5, [UvA geeft steeds hogere cijfers](https://www.at5.nl/nieuws/17014/uva-geeft-steeds-hogere-cijfers)) as the first Dutch university to formally investigate its own grade inflation, but that article wasn't fetched for detail — flag for follow-up if the page needs it.
- **Conclusion for the page**: cijferinflatie is a named, recognized phenomenon in NL higher-ed commentary and is discussed by the Inspectorate/NVAO in passing, but there is no publicly available Inspectorate dataset giving "X% of theses score 9+" nationally. Treat any national percentage as absent, not as "0% / no problem."

## 2. TU Delft cum laude rules and published cum laude rates

- **Cum laude threshold (MSc, university-wide rule, faculty-implemented)**: weighted average of all course grades (excluding the thesis) **≥ 8.00**, AND the graduation/thesis project grade **≥ 9.00**. (Multiple TU Delft RGfBE/RGBoE documents confirm this pattern, e.g. [ME faculty cum laude exceptions & FAQ](https://www.tudelft.nl/en/student/me-student-portal/organisation-me/boards-of-examiners/procedures/cum-laude-exceptions-and-faq), [degrees and exams](https://www.tudelft.nl/en/student/ide/organisation/board-of-examiners/degrees-and-exams).) This is important context: a 9.0 thesis grade is not just "very good," it is literally the *cum laude floor* — TU Delft's own rules assume 9+ theses are supposed to be rare/exceptional, not a third of the cohort.
- **TU Delta (TU Delft's own news outlet), "[Cum laude: faculteiten hanteren eigen regels](https://delta.tudelft.nl/en/article/cum-laude-faculteiten-hanteren-eigen-regels-clone)"**: reports **Industrial Design (IO) cum laude rate held at ~3%**, same as TPM, while Mechanical Engineering (3mE) rose from <3% (2005) to 14% (2015) to 18% (autumn 2016); Architecture rose from <2% (pre-2013/14) to 5.1% (2015-16); one Applied Sciences programme reportedly hit 20% in an earlier year. IO and TPM were reported as targeting ~5% as an informal benchmark. This shows IDE has historically been on the *strict* end of TU Delft faculties for cum laude, which makes a jump to "a third get 9+" a much bigger swing than it would be for a faculty that was already lenient.
- **TU Delta, "[Cum laude afstuderen? Dat gaat overal anders](https://delta.tudelft.nl/en/article/cum-laude-afstuderen-dat-gaat-overal-anders-clone)"** (10 Jan 2017): confirms **IO cum laude rate = 3%** vs. Mechanical Engineering ≈18% (6x higher), and that TU Eindhoven — unlike TU Delft at the time — had already standardized on 8.0 average + 9.0 thesis across all its programmes a decade earlier. Notes criteria "can change per year in principle" per faculty.
- Also surfaced but not independently confirmed by direct quote: TU Delft news coverage describes an IDE annual report characterising its cum laude percentage as *"relatively high, especially in a national context,"* with the Board of Examiners chair stating an increase in cum laude rate "does not necessarily mean a study has become easier," and describing periodic resampling of theses by uninvolved examiners as showing "nearly identical" results to the original grade — i.e. TU Delft's own quality-control position is that its checks have not found systematic over-grading. Treat this quote as reported-via-search-summary, not independently re-verified against the primary annual report PDF; worth pulling the actual IDE Board of Examiners jaarverslag before quoting on the page.
- **No IDE-specific published breakdown of "% of graduation projects scoring 9 or higher" was located.** The cum laude rate (3%, historically) is not the same statistic as "9+" since cum laude requires BOTH an 8.0 average AND a 9.0 thesis — so the "9+ on the thesis alone" rate could be considerably higher than the cum laude rate even historically. This is a real gap: the supervisor's "a third now get 9+" claim cannot currently be checked against any published IDE number; it needs to come from IDE's own MyCase/graduation-manual grade records.

DfI/IPD/SPD), panel by AeQui: `ACCR_008900_21PH-56955_Industrieel_Ontwerpen_Rapport_2020.pdf`, published at [publicaties.nvao.net](https://publicaties.nvao.net/ACCR_008900_21PH-56955_Industrieel_Ontwerpen_Rapport_2020.pdf) (also indexed at [nvao.net/nl/besluiten/technische-universiteit-delft/m-design-for-interaction](https://www.nvao.net/nl/besluiten/technische-universiteit-delft/m-design-for-interaction)). No numeric grade-distribution or "too many high grades" finding in the report — but the panel's qualitative critique is directly relevant to the rubric-redesign rationale:
  - *"The committee noticed that there is no indication of the relative weighting of the individual exam components. According to the programme representatives, the motivation of the respective marks is taken into account when deciding on the final grade."*
  - *"[They] confirmed that there is no direct relationship between the assessment form and the objectives of the bachelor/master final assignment or the overall programme learning outcomes."*
  - On the scoring matrix used by one master track: *"this matrix ... provided a useful framework for setting marks but did not refer to the thesis objectives or intended programme learning outcomes."*
  - Only one evaluation form was filed per student despite 2-3 examiners signing it, so the panel could not verify each assessor's individual input.
  - This is essentially the NVAO naming the *structural* cause a supervisor-marking-led, weakly-anchored rubric produces: marks not tied to explicit objective-level criteria, and no auditable individual assessor input — exactly the disease the new points-based (2/4/7/11 per objective) rubric is designed to cure.
- No comparable numeric "grade distribution" language found in NVAO reports for DfI/IPD/SPD individually; the 2020 report covers the whole IO department including all three master's tracks and the bachelor's programme jointly.

## 4. International evidence on capstone/thesis grading leniency

- **Supervisor leniency is a well-documented, general finding**, not Dutch-specific:
  - [Leniency and halo effects in marking undergraduate short research projects](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC538755/) — found supervisors mark roughly **half a grade higher** than independent second markers on average; leniency effect confirmed, halo effect not.
  - [The use and influence of supervisor marking in graduate research project assessment: a scoping review (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2026.2619903) — scoping review of the literature on supervisor-as-assessor bias in graduate research projects.
  - [Control-deficit leniency bias in work-integrated learning assessment (2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02602938.2025.2539288) — argues assessors default to leniency specifically when they have limited oversight/control of the work being assessed (directly analogous to a supervisor grading a project they were embedded in).
  - [Can professors buy better evaluation with lenient grading? (2020)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2020.1821866) — grade inflation linked to incentive effects on student evaluations of teaching.
- **Commonly cited mechanisms** (from the above + general grade-inflation literature, e.g. the [Wikipedia grade inflation overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_inflation) as a pointer, not a primary source): fear of damaging the supervisory relationship, fear of a negative emotional reaction from the student, concern that a low grade reflects badly on the supervisor's own teaching/guidance, and — specific to capstone/thesis work — the assessor (supervisor) having much deeper, longer-term investment in the outcome than an arm's-length examiner would.
- No specific Bettinger citation could be located and verified in the time available — omit that name from the page rather than risk misattribution.

## 5. What the TU Delft grade scale is supposed to mean

TU Delft's own published grading-scale documents ([MSc PDF](https://filelist.tudelft.nl/TUDelft/Onderwijs/Toelating_en_aanmelding/Exchange/updated_files/TU%20Delft%20grading%20scale%20MSc%20students.pdf)) give both the descriptor table and, critically, an **actual measured distribution**:

| Mark | Definition |
|---|---|
| 9.5–10.0 | Excellent |
| 8.5–9.0 | Very good |
| 7.5–8.0 | Good |
| 6.5–7.0 | More than satisfactory |
| 6.0 | Satisfactory (pass) |
| 4.5–5.5 | Nearly satisfactory |
| 3.5–4.0 | Unsatisfactory |
| 1.0–3.0 | Very poor |

Document text: *"The Dutch grading culture is rather conservative: a 10, or even a 9, is not often awarded. At TU Delft even higher standards are the norm."* — immediately followed, in the same document, by TU Delft's own **measured distribution of MSc graduates' average results, September 2019–August 2022**:

| Grade | % of graduates |
|---|---|
| 10 | 4.0% |
| 9 | 27.0% |
| 8 | 41.1% |
| 7 | 21.5% |
| 6 | 6.2% |
| 5 | 0.2% |

**This is the single most important hard number found: TU Delft-wide, 31.0% of MSc graduates' *weighted average results* are already 9 or higher (10s + 9s).** That is university-wide across all faculties (not IDE-specific, and it's the *average of all course grades*, not the thesis grade alone — though the cum laude rule ties the two together: an 8.0 average is the cum laude floor, and this table shows 31% already clear that on their *overall* average). This both (a) shows the "about a third" figure the supervisor cites is in the right ballpark for TU Delft as a whole, even if not for IDE's thesis grade specifically, and (b) directly contradicts TU Delft's own stated self-image ("a 9 is not often awarded") — the university's own data shows 9s+ are now closer to a plurality outcome than a rare one. This is strong, quotable, directly-sourced evidence for the page.

## 6. How the new IDE rubric's points-to-grade mapping would plausibly shape the distribution

**Assumptions (stated explicitly, since I was not given the rubric document itself — only the numbers in the task):**
- 8 graduation objectives, each independently rated at one of 4 levels worth **2 / 4 / 7 / 11 points**.
- Max total = 8 × 11 = 88 points → grade 10.0. Minimum pass total = 32 points → grade ~5.8–6.0 (task gives "32 points = 6.0 pass" and, separately, "piecewise linear from (32, 5.8) to (88, 10)" — I used the (32, 5.8)→(88,10) linear mapping for all calculations below, since that's the more precisely specified anchor pair; if the real cutoff is exactly (32, 6.0) the numbers below shift by only a few tenths).
- Linear mapping: `grade = 5.8 + 0.075 × (total − 32)`, since slope = (10 − 5.8) / (88 − 32) = 4.2 / 56 = 0.075 grade points per rubric point.

**Computed grades for the requested totals:**

| Total points | Composition (equal weights) | Computed grade |
|---|---|---|
| 56 | All 8 objectives at level 3 (7 pts each) | **7.6** |
| 64 | 8 pts above all-level-3 (e.g. 2 objectives bumped to level 4) | **8.2** |
| 72 | 16 pts above baseline (e.g. 4 objectives at level 4, 4 at level 3) | **8.8** |
| 80 | 24 pts above baseline (e.g. 6 objectives at level 4, 2 at level 3) | **9.4** |

**What mix yields exactly a 9.0?** Solving `9.0 = 5.8 + 0.075(x − 32)` gives **x ≈ 74.7 points**. With levels 7 (level 3) and 11 (level 4) per objective and k objectives at level 4: total = 56 + 4k, so k ≈ 4.7 — i.e., **roughly 5 of the 8 objectives need to be rated at the top level ("excellent"), with the remaining 3 at "good," to reach a 9.0.** A clean "all level-4" thesis maxes at 10.0; a "half level-3, half level-4" thesis (total 72) lands at 8.8, just under a 9.

**Reading for the page**: under this mapping, a 9+ is *not* a low bar — it requires raters to award the top rubric level on a clear majority (5/8) of objectives, not just "mostly good with a couple of highlights." If assessors are in fact producing top-level ratings on 5+ of 8 objectives for roughly a third of projects, that points to **rater calibration/leniency** (exactly the mechanism documented in §4, and the same "marks not tied to explicit objective-level criteria" critique the NVAO panel made in §3) rather than the rubric's arithmetic itself being the driver — the arithmetic, as specified, is actually fairly demanding in the 8.8→9.4 range (each extra objective bumped from level 3 to level 4 is worth 0.3 grade points). This is worth stating explicitly on the page: the new rubric's math does not by itself explain a 33% 9-rate; if that rate materializes, the lever to pull is calibration/moderation of what counts as "level 4 on an objective," not the points-to-grade formula.

**Caveat**: this is a reasoned estimate from the numbers given in the task, not a verified transcription of the actual IDE rubric document — recommend checking the real point values/weights and exact pass-point anchor before publishing the table on the page.
